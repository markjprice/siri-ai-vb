# What Apple and John Ternus meant

At Apple's September 9, 2026, event, John Ternus argued that useful AI becomes more personal when it brings together a person's life with the apps, services, and products used every day. He called the product at the center of those experiences an **intelligent personal hub**.

His thought experiment started with the qualities such a hub would need, as described in the following table:

| Quality | What it means in practice | Apple capability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Always with you | The hub can help during small moments that would not justify opening a laptop. | iPhone, Apple Watch, AirPods, CarPlay |
| Personal context | It can connect relationships, routines, messages, email, photos, calendar items, and onscreen content. | Siri AI personal context, Spotlight index, onscreen awareness |
| Local intelligence | It can answer some requests quickly without sending everything to a server. | Apple Foundation Models on device |
| Cloud reach | It can use current web information and larger models when a request needs more capability. | Networking and Private Cloud Compute |
| Eyes and ears | It can reason over text, images, scenes, and speech. | Cameras, microphones, Visual Intelligence, Dictation |
| A rich display | It can show sources, plans, maps, images, controls, and confirmations. | iPhone display; iPhone Duo inner and outer displays |
| All-day availability | It remains useful through work, travel, exercise, and home life. | Battery, mobile networking, satellite features where supported |
| Low learning curve | It uses familiar apps, voice, touch, and ordinary language. | Siri, Search or Ask, Shortcuts, system app actions |
| Ecosystem connection | It passes work and context among the products already in use. | iCloud, Continuity, Handoff, AirDrop, Universal Clipboard, Apple Watch, AirPods, Home, CarPlay |
| Privacy as architecture | Personal context is processed on device when possible, with protected cloud processing for more complex requests. | On-device processing, Private Cloud Compute, permissions, Apple Intelligence Report |

Ternus's phrase deliberately echoes Steve Jobs's **digital hub** strategy for the Mac. The old hub collected and managed digital devices such as cameras and music players. The new hub centers a person rather than a collection of peripherals. It coordinates information, sensors, services, and AI around the user's current situation.

## Why iPhone Duo could be the best version of the hub

The Duo's strongest argument is not simply **more screen**. It is **more context without losing presence**.

An iPad gives you a large canvas, but it is not always in your pocket. A Mac is better for sustained writing, file management, and complex production, but it does not travel through every small moment of the day. A conventional iPhone is always present, but it frequently makes you switch back and forth between the thing you are looking at and the assistant helping you with it.

The Duo combines the phone's constant presence with a temporary work surface:

- Keep Siri AI on one side and the source on the other. This makes checking an answer against an email, message, document, map, or web page easier.
- Save app pairs for repeated contexts, such as Siri plus Calendar, Maps plus Notes, Health plus Reminders, or Safari plus a destination AI.
- Open two windows of the same app for comparison, such as two Safari pages when shopping or checking travel options.
- Use the outer display for quick capture and glanceable information, then unfold only when a task requires comparison, planning, or review.
- Prop it in laptop or tent form for calls, translation, recording, media, and StandBy without a separate stand.
- Use the rear cameras for higher-quality selfies with an outer-display preview, or let another person see the framing.
- Use Apple Pencil for annotation and sketching when the inner screen makes that practical.
- Run on-device models with the A20 Pro and dual Neural Engine while the vapor chamber sustains heavier work.

The theme of using iPhone Duo as your intelligence personal hub is **glance, unfold, hand off**:

1. **Glance** on the outer screen when the hub only needs to show or capture one thing.
2. **Unfold** when you need the source and the assistant, two apps, or a larger review surface.
3. **Hand off** to the Mac or iPad when the task becomes sustained creation rather than mobile decision-making.

This avoids the false promise that a foldable phone should replace every other computer. It also fits the likely workflow of anyone who writes: capture and organize ideas on iPhone, inspect sources on the Duo's inner display, then continue the writing and editing on Mac.

# Your iPhone already sits in the middle

Think about the last ordinary hour of your day. Your iPhone may have shown the next appointment, carried a message from someone you care about, stored a photograph, remembered where you parked, unlocked a door, counted a walk, paid for lunch, and handed a web page to your Mac. You probably did not think of any of those actions as parts of one system.

Apple now has a name for that system: the **intelligent personal hub**.

The name does not appear beneath an icon on your Home Screen. It describes the role the iPhone can play when its apps, sensors, services, and connected devices work together. Siri AI adds a conversational way to reach that system. Instead of remembering which app contains a detail, you can describe what you need. Instead of copying the detail into another app, you can ask Siri to prepare the next action.

The word **personal** matters as much as **intelligent**. A general chatbot may know more about the world, but it does not automatically know that the person named Elena in a message is your sister, that the address on a pass is where you need to be tomorrow, or that the photograph on your screen contains a date worth adding to Calendar. Your iPhone can connect those clues because they already exist in the life you have entrusted to it.

That access creates responsibility. A hub that can find a private message, inspect a screen, control a home, or prepare a reply can also expose the wrong information or take the wrong action. This bonus book therefore follows one rule from beginning to end: Siri AI may gather, explain, and prepare, but you review before anything consequential happens.

You do not need every Apple product to build a useful hub. Start with the iPhone you already have. Add a Watch, AirPods, Mac, iPad, HomePod, car, or iPhone Duo only where it solves a real problem. The goal is not to surround yourself with devices. It is to make the devices already around you work as one understandable system.

# A larger surface that still fits in your pocket

The ordinary iPhone is a strong personal hub because it is always nearby. Its weakness is that a phone screen often makes you choose between the source and the assistant. You look at an email, switch to Siri, return to the email to check a date, then switch back again to act.

iPhone Duo changes that interaction when you unfold it. Put Siri AI on one side and the source on the other. Keep Maps beside the note that contains an address. Compare two Safari pages. Review a calendar change while the message that requested it remains visible. The larger display does not make Siri more accurate, but it makes your checking easier.

That distinction matters. The Duo is not automatically a better hub because it costs more or has a larger screen. It is better when the task benefits from two visible contexts, a hands-free folding position, or a canvas large enough to inspect before acting. For a one-sentence reminder, leave it closed. For a quick answer, use the outer display. Unfold it when seeing more will help you make a better decision.

Use this rule:

- **Glance** for one fact or one small action.
- **Unfold** for comparison, planning, or review.
- **Hand off** for sustained creation on a Mac or iPad.

The best personal hub is not the device that tries to do everything. It is the device that helps you recognize what should happen next and move the task to the right place.
